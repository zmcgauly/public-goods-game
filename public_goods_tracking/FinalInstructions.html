This experiment is about economic decision making. During the experiment, your decisions and the decisions of other participants will help determine how much money you may earn. You will receive a show-up fee of {{ show_up_fee }} for participating. This show-up fee is separate from any additional money you may earn during the experiment, and you will receive it regardless of the decisions you make.

If you participate in more than one test today, you will receive only one $10 show-up payment total.

The experiment consists of ten rounds grouped into five periods. Each period contains two rounds with the same group. Each round has two stages: the main stage and the elicitation stage. After all ten rounds are completed, you will complete a short survey. Once both the ten rounds and the survey are complete, one of the five periods will be randomly selected for payment. The elicitation-stage earnings from both rounds in that selected period will be reported to you, and the experiment will conclude.

In the main stage of each round, you will receive 10 balls. These balls are only used for that round. In the next round, you will receive 10 new balls. You will be placed in a group of three players and asked how many of your 10 balls you want to contribute to a shared group account. You may enter any whole number from 0 to 10. The contributions from all three players in your group will be added together, multiplied by 1.6, and divided evenly among the three players. Balls you do not contribute are not added to the shared account, but they also do not automatically become payment for you. The group account calculation is part of the decision-making task, but it will not be paid to you.

After the main stage, you will complete the elicitation stage. In this stage, you will make guesses about how much the other two players in your group contributed to the shared account. Since each player has 10 balls, each contribution must be a whole number from 0 to 10. You will place balls on the contribution amounts you think are most likely. You may put more balls on one amount if you think it is especially likely, or you may spread your balls across multiple amounts if you are unsure. If the period containing that round is selected for payment, each ball you placed on the correct contribution amount for another player earns one point, worth ${{ C.POINT_DOLLAR_VALUE }} in real-life dollars.

Your group will stay the same for two rounds at a time. After every two rounds, groups will be reshuffled, and you may be assigned new groupmates. Because your groupmates may change, the players you are asked about in the elicitation stage may also change. After all players complete both stages, the experiment will move to the next round, or, after the tenth round and survey are complete, to the payment selection and conclusion.
